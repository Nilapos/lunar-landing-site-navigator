# Moonwalk: Lunar Landing Site Navigator

Our project for the [NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/lunar-landing-site-navigator/), challenge "Lunar Landing Site Navigator".

**Live demo:** https://lunar-landing-site-navigator-beta.vercel.app (desktop browser; best in Chrome or Edge with WebGL)

## What it does

Mission planners, educators and the public can compare landing sites and landing dates in the **lunar south polar region (80°S to the pole)** by seeing **where the Sun and the Earth are relative to the local horizon, terrain included**:

- **Power**: sunlight windows, longest darkness, time before sunset.
- **Direct-to-Earth communication**: Earth visibility windows, time before Earthset.
- **Terrain**: slopes around the site and the zones a rover cannot drive.

Every site gets a green / orange / red verdict and four scores out of 100 (Solar Exposure, Telecom, Window Duration, Topography) plus a Global Score, computed against the rover requirements you type in.

## A 2-minute tour

1. **Sky** (opens first): the curved south polar cap with the Sun and the Earth in the sky at their true direction and angular size. The ground is painted with layers (sunlight and shadows, Earth visibility, forbidden zones) that follow the timeline. One card shows the score if you land at the moment shown. Switch between **Orbit** and **Top view**: the camera flies from one to the other. Move over the ground to judge a 2.4 km cell; click a pin to analyze a site.
2. **On site**: stand on the selected site and see its real terrain skyline, with the Sun and the Earth above or behind it.
3. **Why**: for the site and the moment shown, why the Sun and the Earth are visible or hidden: sky chart, cause strip (visible / hidden by terrain / below the flat horizon) and a ray diagram.
4. **Analysis**: key figures and charts for the scenario, and an A/B comparison (two sites, or one site at two dates).
5. **Sites**: every site on the map and ranked by score.
6. **Timeline** (bottom): Play, step by hour / day / week. The score card always shows what the scores would be if you land at the moment shown; **Land here** makes that moment the landing time.
7. **Settings** (foot of the left rail): site, landing date, rover requirements and the **Pitch demo** button (Malapert massif summit, 14 Nov 2026). Landing at 00:00 gives a window score of 0 (global 58); waiting until about 15 Nov 06:00 UT gives 100 (global 83), with the default rover requirements. **Missions** (below it) lists the missions to the south pole.

## How the scores work

One rule everywhere: `ratio = what the site offers / what the rover needs`, `score = 100 x min(ratio / 2, 1)` (50 means the requirement is just met, 100 means at least twice the margin); green from ratio 1.2, orange between 1 and 1.2, red below 1. The Global Score is the average of the four. The rover requirements in the left panel are placeholders to adapt.

## Data and sources

- **Sun and Earth positions**: NASA JPL Horizons (queried server-side by `/api/horizons` and `/api/sky`).
- **Terrain, slopes, horizon**: LOLA elevation map of the south pole, 80 m per pixel (NASA PDS). The 115 MB source map is not in the repository; the derived data are.
- **Sites and missions**: every coordinate has a source next to it in `src/data/`. NASA's official Artemis III region centers are not in the sources we could open, so those entries are representative points, flagged as approximate.

## Limits (said honestly)

- The ground layers use 2.4 km cells: an overview that can differ from the precise per-site analysis on summits and slopes near the pole.
- Sun and Earth are treated as points (no partial sunrises).
- Textures of the Earth, the Sun's glare and the Moon outside the mapped cap are generated by code: stylized. Only the positions and angular sizes of the bodies are real.
- The skyline in the site view is the site's real LOLA horizon; the ground drawn below it is not.

## Run it

Requires [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 24 and Git.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # local app, with the API routes
npm test         # unit tests
npm run lint
npm run build
```

Folders: `src/components/` (UI), `src/lib/` (logic, tested), `src/data/` (sites, missions, precomputed terrain), `api/` (serverless functions), `scripts/` (data builds). See [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) for how the data pipeline and the code fit together.
